- Add a heading like "What results do COAI clients report?" instead of a generic label

### 2. "You're already paying for leads. You're just not catching them." — Score: 53/100
**Current opening:** > You're already paying for leads. You're just not catching them. Pain #1: Missed calls while you're on a roof or in an attic. Your competitor answers first. That job is gone.

**Problem:** Heavy pronoun reliance ("you/your" appears 12 times). Narrative-driven pain points with no definitional openings. The calculation at the end ($8,000/month) is strong but buried after three paragraphs of persuasive copy. An AI engine cannot extract a clean answer block from this section.

**Suggested rewrite:** > **How much revenue do Bakersfield trades lose from missed calls?** Bakersfield plumbing, HVAC, and electrical contractors lose an estimated $8,000 per month from unanswered phone calls. The formula: 5 missed calls per week × $400 average job value × 4 weeks = $3,200–$8,000 in recoverable revenue. The three primary causes are: (1) missed calls during active jobs, (2) website platforms like Wix and GoDaddy that lack missed-call text-back, and (3) poor Google Maps visibility that allows competitors to capture local searches.

**Additional improvements:**
- Lead with the calculation, not the pain narrative
- Replace "you/your" with named subjects: "Bakersfield contractors", "Kern County trades"
- Add a summary table: Cause | Impact | Recoverable Revenue
- Make the $104,000/year figure prominent, not buried

### 3. "A former contractor who builds digital systems." — Score: 60/100

## Category Deep-Dive

### Answer Block Quality (60/100)
The page has a split personality. The FAQ section (92/100) and Comparison Table (78/100) are citability gold — direct Q&A format, extractable answers, definitional structures. But the Hero (45), Pain Points (35), Testimonials (30), and CTA (48) are narrative-driven persuasion copy with no answer blocks. For AI citability, the page needs 60%+ of sections to open with extractable answer patterns. Currently only 5 of 11 blocks score above 60 on this dimension.

**Critical gap:** The Hero section — the first thing AI engines read — has no definitional opening. An AI asked "What is COAI Bakersfield?" cannot extract a clean answer from "Get More Job Calls Without Spending More on Ads."

### Passage Self-Containment (76/100)
The strongest dimension. Bulleted lists (TradeCall System, Features) and the Comparison Table are highly self-contained. The Pain Points section (40) drags the score down with heavy "you/your" pronoun reliance — 12 instances in 130 words. If a paragraph is extracted from this section, "you" has no antecedent.

### Structural Readability (76/100)
Good hierarchy with H1 > H2 > H3 structure. The Comparison Table (92) and FAQ (90) are format leaders. Missing: question-based headings on 6 of 11 sections. The only question headings are "How much are missed calls costing you?" and the FAQ. Adding questions like "What is the TradeCall System?" and "Who built COAI?" would lift this score significantly.

### Statistical Density (63/100)
Uneven distribution. The ROI Calculator (95), Features (88), and Pain Points (85) sections are stat-rich. The About section (15) and Testimonials (10) are stat-free. The page has 11 countable statistics across ~825 words of content — roughly 1.3 per 500 words, placing it in the 50-69 tier. To reach 70+, the page needs 3-4 stats per 500 words consistently.

**Available but underused stats:** The $104,000/year figure is mentioned once and buried. The 12+ sites built is mentioned twice but never with examples. The 5.0 Google rating appears once in the hero trust badges.

### Uniqueness & Original Data (65/100)
The founder's contractor-to-developer story (82) and proprietary TradeCall System naming (68) are genuine differentiators. The ROI calculation (75) and Comparison Table (72) provide original analysis. But the Testimonials (60) and CTA (35) are generic. The page lacks first-party research, case study data, or proprietary benchmarks that would make it a citation source rather than a service page.
